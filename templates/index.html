How it works

From raw review text to an objective lexical classification in four deterministic steps.

01

Paste Review

Accepts customer feedback, buyer testimonials, or third-party product comments as input strings.

02

Text Cleaning

Normalizes characters, scrubs HTML tags, removes raw URLs, handles duplicate entries, and strips punctuation.

03

TF-IDF Vectorization

Extracts up to 30,000 unigram and bigram token features to map statistical word importance across the corpus.

04

Discrete Prediction

Linear SVM evaluates the feature vector and outputs exactly one label: Likely Genuine or Potentially Fake.

    01 / The Challenge

    The Problem of Fake Reviews

    In contemporary online commerce, customer purchasing decisions rely heavily on peer testimonials. However, the proliferation of incentivized fake reviews and computer-generated synthetic feedback undermines marketplace transparency.

    ReviewTrellis was developed as an exploratory Machine Learning research prototype to analyze linguistic distributions within reviews, utilizing robust lexical n-gram statistics to flag reviews that exhibit markers consistent with synthetic generation.

    03 / Algorithms

    Evaluated Classifiers

    Three supervised algorithms tested under identical 80/20 train/test splits.

    Linear Model Baseline

    Logistic Regression

    Estimates the probability of binary outcomes using the logistic sigmoid function over linear weighted feature combinations. Highly interpretable baseline with fast convergence.

    Regularization: L2 penalty · Solver: lbfgs
    Probabilistic Frequency-based

    Multinomial Naive Bayes

    Applies Bayes' theorem assuming conditional independence between token frequencies given the review class. Exceptional speed and well suited for sparse word-frequency representations.

    Smoothing: Laplace alpha=1.0

    Max-Margin Best Test Results

    Linear Support Vector Machine

    Finds the optimal separating hyperplane that maximizes the geometric margin between genuine and fake text vectors in the 30,000-dimensional TF-IDF sparse space.

    Loss: Squared Hinge · C=1.0 · Max iter: 2000

    05 / Reference

    Evaluation Metrics Explained

    Clear definitions of how classifier health is measured.

    Accuracy

    The ratio of correctly classified reviews (both genuine and fake) over the total number of evaluated samples. Useful on balanced distributions.

    Precision

    Of all reviews flagged by the model as Potentially Fake, how many were genuinely synthetic. High precision minimizes false alarms against legitimate buyers.

    Recall (Sensitivity)

    Of all actual computer-generated reviews in the test split, what proportion did the model successfully catch. High recall ensures fewer fake reviews slip through unnoticed.

    F1-Score

    The harmonic mean of Precision and Recall. Balances both trade-offs into a unified benchmark score for model selection.

    Confusion Matrix

    A 2x2 contingency grid comparing True Labels versus Predicted Labels (True Positives, False Positives, True Negatives, False Negatives), exposing directional bias.

    06 / Scope & Limitations

    Dataset Origin & Ethical Boundaries

    The training pipeline was trained upon a publicly accessible benchmark dataset contrasting verified consumer product reviews (Original) against algorithmic text generator reviews (Computer-Generated).

    Because language generation constantly evolves, lexical n-grams (TF-IDF) identify statistical vocabulary density rather than verifying semantic ground truth or physical purchase records. Consequently, all system results are explicitly presented as Likely Genuine or Potentially Fake, reinforcing that ML predictions serve as advisory signals rather than absolute determinations.
